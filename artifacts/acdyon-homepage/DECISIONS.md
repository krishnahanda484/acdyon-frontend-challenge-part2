# Decisions

## 1. Why this track and approach

I chose Part 2, the premium homepage, because it is the clearest way to show product thinking, visual craft, and frontend judgment without taking on the risk of scraping a protected platform. The supplied AcdyOn material points toward practitioner-led learning, real-world expertise, and future-ready capability, so I shaped the page around a simple promise: turn the next thing you need to know into something you can do.

The story follows one learning journey — Orient, Learn, Apply, Grow — rather than presenting a collection of disconnected marketing sections. The journey selector is the one meaningful product interaction. It gives the visitor a concrete sense of how learning could work while keeping the rest of the page calm and editorial.

## 2. Trade-off

I prioritized one polished, responsive homepage over a multi-page marketing site or a simulated enrollment flow. The preview workspace is intentionally labelled as illustrative, and the final call to action leads back to the journey instead of pretending that a live catalogue, account system, or programme intake already exists.

The page uses AcdyOn’s supplied brand context and the official logo asset, but the visual system is a candidate redesign concept rather than an official production page. If this moved forward, I would validate the direction with AcdyOn, replace the concept workspace with approved product screens, connect programme content to a CMS, and wire the call to action to a real conversion event.

## 3. AI use and verification

AI helped accelerate implementation and explore presentation options, but I made the final product and engineering decisions. I checked the supplied assessment and brief, kept the copy grounded in the available AcdyOn context, removed unsupported testimonials, numbers, partnerships, ratings, and success claims, and labelled invented interface content as illustrative.

I also verified the implementation with a production build, direct local preview, responsive viewport checks, keyboard focus review, and browser-console review. The result is intentionally dependency-light and readable in a follow-up code review.
